# notes 2 self

I am on war with inspirational quotes. It is hard to say so, because it seems like, I've been dependent on them for quite a while.
And it is also difficult to say, that scrolling through possibly motivating (but mostly entertaining) videos on Youtube is just
a waste of time. When I would be honest, my life would not have been what it is now, when I would not have randomly stumbled
over Jordan Peterson videos. It feels truly uncomfortable to admit, that my life changed with watching some professor on the
internet bringing back religious values. One could say, that those are merely some photons hitting my retina, getting processed
by the brain together with appropriate noise consumption. But hey, that reductionist point of view would just as well be true
for every "real" conversation. So it can not be this simple.
Still, a part of me prevails, that is drawn to such motivating content while another is skeptical.
I think there are some truths in both perspectives. On the one hand, one cannot create his own values. How we act is probably
connected to what we want to achieve. What we want to achieve (i.e. our goals in broadest sense) are weighted by our values.
Where do we get our values from? How do we update our values? Are some of these values inscribed in our deepest self?
Do we have to consume stories, that tell us how to live in the world? Furthermore, we are not machines. When parts of
our nervous system wants something or wants something not, it is not trivial to convince it for the (what we deem) better.
That is, maybe we have to give our conscious some food to drive on. But maybe, this can be filtered.

On the other hand, much of consumption probably is a waste of time. Something that feels good. Something that we do not
have to do anything for, a dopamine kick for nothing. I am highly suspicous of that. Furthermore, it even seems like
everything has to be entertainment. We consume informative content because either it is prepared to be entertaining
or we become a superficial notion of the topic at hand with which we can go out in the world and brag about. "New" things
are very unlikely to remain, whereas old themes keep coming back.

Nowadays I am not so sure about my critique of social media and the all interrupting smart phone usage.
I have an intuition, that it is in general not beneficial. To have to use the smartphone to look everything up
seems like a very brittle foundation of knowledge. But on the other hand, I do not have the impression that younger
people who seem to use their smart phones more often are talking less with each otheer, having less real-life
physical interactions. Even more, me not being particularly social, I do not necessarily have the impression that
I get more talkative the less I use these devices. It is not conclusive to me. Indeed, what is conclusive, is
that I should not feel to have the upper hand, a certain moral superiority just because I use these devices less.
If I am of the opinion that overconsumption of these devices is not good, I should not overconsume them. That is it.
Everything else again seeems like a distraction to not get into the weeds, to not do the hard work of actually grasping
something, to actually have a difficult conversation, to actually do the run.
Success in my domain is probably much more linked to the absolute time I spend with the problems. Not how fasted I am,
how motivated I am, how much I meditated, etc. I think I have to get into states that are uncomfortable but important,
get used to them. It only takes so many trials until I will get used to it and the process to there is
indicated by me not wanting to do it but knowing deep down, that it actually is important.
