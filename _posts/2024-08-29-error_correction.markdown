# Error Correction

I've put together some pieces in my head, particularly by James Smith's reading of David Deutsch and Karl Popper.
It, according to Smith, seems that every learning is error correction. It seems false that knowledge can
simply flow from one mind to another (cf Sokrates' remark on exactly that). Rather, knowledge has to be born
again in the mind of the listener. He has to grapple with it, peak holes in it, test it. Otherwise it may
simply remain words that are ready for recitation but not absorbed into the other's mind.

That may be why it is so important to write and communicate. With that, we "put our ideas out there". Out
there for critique, for feedback. Thoughts remaining in our head might remain untested by the world.
They have to be put into practice otherwise they have no skin in the game. Then it might not even matter
how stupid our ideas seem. What else is there to do than to correct them? One has to muster up the
courage to speak truthfully. Stating some facts that one imagines others would like to hear is common
but helps no one. One simply tests a theory with it that one does not even have stake in. One error corrects
something completely artificial.

Even Feynman noted, that what he cannot create, he does not understand. Naming every piece of a birds anatomy
does not make it a flying bird. The ensembling is what makes it a meaningful creature.

We might even get more radical. Maybe we can even error correct emotions. Maybe thats what therapy, journaling
and all those things are for. Yes you can decorate them with scientified, blown up theories. That is not to say
that those theories are meaningless. If the lowest piece in the puzzle is mere error correction it is by
no means certain that all greater building blocks made up by it are trivial. There must be emergent
phenomena, intricate interactions. For god's sake, we are not even able to analitically describe the motion
of 3 interacting bodies.

Yet, we cling to our beliefs. We cling to our beliefs about the world, about other people, about religion,
about politics, about the market about life, the universe and everything. We can embrace the fact, that
we're just uneducated about most topics and strive to be more knowledgeable this day than yesterday.

We're unbelievable good in not confronting uncomfortable situations. People will start wars out of boredom
if one believes Dostojewski. Yet, say, to get better at something we probably just have to do it a lot
with the intention to get better at it. That is, to error correct! But this at some point also means to
step into unkown territory otherwise there will be no errors. And hence, no learning.
